    Research, comparison, and the recommendation

    What the Pomodoro research says

    • One task, one sprint. Pick a single specific task before the clock starts; do not switch mid-sprint.
    • 25 / 5 is the default, not a rule. A 15–50 minute range is normal; longer blocks suit deep work, shorter blocks suit admin or low resistance.
    • Four sprints, then a long break. 15–30 minutes after four focus intervals. The long break is part of the method, not a bonus.
    • A sprint is indivisible. Capture an interruption and return to it later; if it cannot wait, the sprint is void and restarts.
    • Estimate and review in sprints. Record the task, the sprints it took, and interruptions; use that to calibrate future plans.
    • Keep setup separate from the clock. Show the sequence before Start; do not silently change a running sprint; do not auto-start the next work interval after a break.

    Sources: Duke ARC, Wikipedia, Todoist, Oregon State, Asana, and ScreensDesign on focus-timer settings.

    What changes in the data model

    • No per-task timer fields. A session is a separate record: { taskId, phase, startedAt, endsAt, sprintMin, completed }.
    • Task gets only a focus history summary (focusMin, sprints) after a session ends.
    • Real app should store endsAt timestamps, not interval ticks, so sleep and reloads stay accurate.
